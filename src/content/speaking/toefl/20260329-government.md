## Task 2 — Interview Practice

### Set 1: Government and Public Services

You have volunteered for a research study about government services and basic public procedures. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

---

#### Q1

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S1_Q1.mp3"></audio>

Thank you for participating today. I'd like to ask you some questions about government services. First, **have you or someone you know ever had to deal with a complicated government process, such as applying for a document or registering for a service? What was the experience like?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

Yes, last summer my grandfather tried to apply for his pension online. The website kept showing errors when we uploaded his ID card. We spent three whole hours just trying to reset his password. But the email with the new password never came to his inbox. My grandfather felt very stressed and almost gave up on the money. He said he was too old to use these new computer things. In the end, we had to visit the office in person. We waited in a long line there for more than one hour. Many older people in Taiwan also have the same big problem with these websites.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

I recently helped my grandfather apply for his pension online and it was a total disaster due to the technical barriers. The website was incredibly glitchy and we kept getting server errors whenever we tried to upload his identification documents. This digital exclusion is a huge issue because my grandfather does not own a computer or have high-speed internet, so he felt completely isolated and dependent on me. We spent three hours just trying to reset a password that never arrived in his email, and the mental fatigue from decoding all that technical jargon made him want to give up on his benefits entirely.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I actually had a fantastic experience recently when I used a new government mobile app to renew my professional license. The efficiency of the modern system saved me a fortune on fuel and parking since I didn't have to drive to a physical office during peak hours. What I appreciated most was the real-time tracking bar that showed precisely when my application moved from the review stage to the approval stage. This transparency really reduced my anxiety because I could see the state infrastructure working in real-time. On top of that, the digital receipt was sent instantly to my phone, which allowed me to share it with my employer immediately and prove that I was compliant with all regulations.

#### Q2

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S1_Q2.mp3"></audio>

Great. When you need to complete government tasks, such as renewing an ID or filing paperwork, **do you prefer handling everything online or by mail, or do you like going to an office and dealing with a person face to face? Why?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I prefer online services very much. It saves a lot of time because I can apply at night. I do not need to take a day off from work. I can also finish the form in less than twenty minutes. It also saves money because I do not need to drive downtown. I do not have to pay for gas or expensive parking near the office. Many people in Taiwan also use this method every day. My friends and family all fill out forms on their phones now. So overall, I really think online services are the best choice.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I choose to file everything online because digital systems provide much better accuracy and record-keeping. The biggest thing for me is the instant feedback; if I make a mistake on a digital form, the system flags the error immediately so I can fix it before submitting. This prevents me from getting hit with late fees or having my application rejected weeks later. On top of that, it's just much less stressful because I don't feel the pressure of a long line of people waiting behind me at a desk. I can actually sit at home and read all the fine print very carefully to make sure I understand the legal terms, and I get a confirmation email right away which gives me total peace of mind that my data is safe and secure.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I definitely prefer going to a physical office and dealing with a person face to face to ensure everything is resolved in one sitting. The main reason is that complex bureaucratic issues often involve nuances that a website simply cannot handle. If I have a specific question about my residency status or a confusing document, a trained clerk can provide real-time feedback and clarify instructions through their tone and body language. This human interaction eliminates the weeks of back-and-forth emails you often get with online portals. Plus, it just saves money in the long run because getting expert advice on-site prevents the trial and error of paying multiple application fees for the wrong forms. There is a sense of closure when you hand a document directly to a person and they tell you it is perfect and complete.

#### Q3

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S1_Q3.mp3"></audio>

Interesting. **Some people say government forms should be simplified, even if that means collecting less detailed information. Do you agree or disagree? Why or why not?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I agree with this idea very much. First of all, short forms are much easier to understand for every person. Older people and busy workers do not feel scared of the paper. Simple words also help people who do not speak English well. Also, people are less likely to make small mistakes on a short form. When forms are too long, many people just skip the hard questions. Finally, more people will actually finish and submit their forms on time. This helps the government get better and faster information from everyone. That is why I really think the government should make all forms much shorter.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

I think simplifying forms is a great idea because it actually leads to more accurate information. When forms are too long and complicated, people get frustrated and start making random errors or even skipping sections entirely. This causes a huge problem for the government because they have to spend thousands of hours on manual audits just to fix minor mistakes. By collecting only the most important details, the government gets cleaner data and reduces the back and forth correspondence with citizens. This building of trust means more people will actually participate in a census or apply for social programs since they feel the state is being reasonable and respectful of their time.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I disagree with simplifying these forms because specific and detailed information is essential for proper resource allocation. The biggest thing for me is the precision required to help marginalized groups. If the government only collects general data, they might overlook a specific neighborhood that lacks clean water or specialized medical services. Detailed forms allow planners to identify these niche communities rather than just looking at a single average. Plus, having granular data prevents massive financial fraud by requiring verifiable proof of assets, which ensures that tax dollars actually reach those who truly qualify for assistance rather than being wasted on people who game the system.

#### Q4

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S1_Q4.mp3"></audio>

Good points. Lastly, **some places are introducing digital IDs that people can keep on their phones instead of carrying physical cards. Do you think this is a positive development, or do you think physical IDs should remain the standard? Explain your answer.**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I think digital IDs have very good effects. It saves a lot of time because I always carry my phone. I do not need to carry a plastic card in my wallet. It also saves money because I do not pay for a new plastic card. If I lose my wallet, I do not need to buy a new ID. Many people in Taiwan also choose this way for their daily life. My friends use digital IDs at the bank and the airport now. So overall, I really think digital IDs are a good change.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I believe moving to digital IDs is a positive development because of the advanced security features they offer. The biggest thing for me is biometric protection, like using a fingerprint or face scan to unlock the ID. If someone steals your physical wallet, they have your full name and address immediately, but a thief cannot access a digital ID on a locked phone. On top of that, these apps allow for controlled data sharing. When you go to a club or a movie that has an age limit, you can show a QR code that only confirms you are old enough without revealing your specific birth date or where you live. This prevents identity theft and keeps your personal data private from strangers during everyday transactions.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I prefer sticking with physical IDs as the standard because they are far more reliable and accessible for everyone in society. My main concern is that digital versions require expensive hardware and data plans that many low income individuals simply cannot afford. Plus, physical cards never run out of battery or suffer from software glitches. If you are traveling in a rural area with poor cellular service and your phone dies, you could be left completely stranded at a security checkpoint without any way to prove who you are. The thing is, physical documents work everywhere and do not exclude the elderly who might struggle with complex touchscreens. This ensures that a person's ability to participate in society is not dependent on their tech skills or their ability to buy the newest smartphone every two years.

### Set 2: Volunteering and Community Service

You have volunteered for a research study about community involvement. You will have a short online interview with a researcher. The researcher will ask you some questions.

---

#### Q1

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S2_Q1.mp3"></audio>

Thank you for speaking with me today. I'd like to ask you some questions about volunteering and community service. First, **can you share one or two experiences you or someone you know has had with volunteering or helping in the community?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

Yes, last summer I helped clean a local park with my classmates. We picked up trash from the grass and planted small trees together. We worked very hard for a whole Saturday morning under the hot sun. It felt really good because the park looked much cleaner after one day. Many old people came to thank us with cold drinks and snacks. I also made new friends from other schools during the activity. We still chat on the phone and meet for lunch sometimes. Many students in Taiwan also join this kind of community work on weekends.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I feel that helping the community is mostly about staying healthy. I spent my weekends last summer working in a community garden and it actually replaced my gym membership because the physical labor was so intense. This helped me save money while getting fresh air and lowering my stress levels. The best part was meeting my neighbors because urban life can feel very lonely. We formed deep emotional bonds while working together, and those weekly visits to pull weeds and plant vegetables really gave me a sense of purpose that helped me avoid burning out from my regular office job and helped me feel more connected to the people around me.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

For me, volunteering can actually be quite a burden because of the hidden financial and time costs involved. A student I know had to commute over ten hours a week to reach a remote service site, which ended up costing a fortune in gas and train tickets. This was money she didn't have since she had to quit her part-time job to meet the mandatory service hours for her program. On top of that, the physical exhaustion from building houses all weekend meant she was too tired to study for her professional certifications. This constant pressure caused a lot of strain on her family relationships and led to serious compassion fatigue because she was emotionally drained from working in such high-stress environments.

#### Q2

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S2_Q2.mp3"></audio>

I see. Some volunteer programs are organized by large international organizations, while others are run by small local groups. **Would the type of organization affect your willingness to volunteer? Why or why not?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I prefer small local groups very much. First of all, I can see the real change in my own neighborhood. When I clean a park, I walk by it every day and feel proud. Also, I get to know local people and build real trust with them. Small groups feel like a family, not like a big office. Finally, my help goes straight to the people who need it most. There are no big office fees or expensive ads to pay for. That is why I really think small local groups are the best choice.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I would choose a large organization because of the long-term career benefits and professional networking. These big names carry a lot of prestige on a resume, and they often offer formal training modules that teach you how to use industry-standard software for project management. On top of that, you get to connect with high-level executives and mentors who can write strong recommendation letters for graduate school applications. Being part of a global digital platform also means you can meet other volunteers from all over the world, which builds a massive professional network that helps you find paid internships or job openings in the future.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

For me, working with a small local group is much more appealing because the impact is immediate and deeply personal. When you volunteer locally, there is almost no administrative overhead, so every dollar raised at a neighborhood bake sale goes directly toward buying supplies like food or blankets for people in your own city. You bypass all that frustrating corporate bureaucracy and those endless meetings that large groups require. Instead, you get to build genuine bonds with your neighbors and see the tangible results of your labor right in front of you. This direct feedback loop creates a much stronger sense of community and provides a unique kind of mental fulfillment when you realize you are strengthening the local safety net for everyone around you.

#### Q3

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S2_Q3.mp3"></audio>

Interesting. Some people believe that universities and high schools should require students to complete a certain number of volunteer hours in order to graduate. **Do you think mandatory volunteering is a good strategy for building community? Why or why not?**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I disagree with this idea very much. First of all, students are already very busy with schoolwork every day. They have tests, homework, and after-school classes from morning to late night. Also, forced service feels fake and not meaningful at all. Students will just sign the paper and not really care about helping real people. This kills the kind and happy spirit of volunteering. Finally, it is very unfair to students who have part-time jobs. Many of my friends need to work to help their family with money. That is why I really think schools should never force volunteer work.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I believe mandatory volunteering is a smart move because it teaches students how to be responsible citizens. The biggest thing for me is empathy. When students work with people from different backgrounds, like the homeless or the elderly, it breaks down social barriers and helps them understand problems they might not see in their own lives. It also establishes a long-term habit of giving back. By forcing students to prioritize the needs of others over their own free time, they develop better emotional intelligence and a sense of shared humanity that stays with them throughout their entire adult lives.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I actually disagree with making volunteering a requirement because it creates a massive logistical burden and increases inequality. My main concern is the financial strain on low-income students who often need to work paid jobs to support their families or pay for their tuition. Forcing them to work for free takes away precious hours from their shifts or their study time, which can lead to severe burnout and high stress levels. On top of that, making service a graduation checkbox actually ruins the spirit of altruism. Instead of feeling a genuine connection to the community, students end up feeling resentful toward the organization and treat the whole thing as a pointless obligation.

#### Q4

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp4/speaking/T2_S2_Q4.mp3"></audio>

Good points. Lastly, looking to the future, **do you think the way people volunteer will change? For example, will more volunteering happen online, or will in-person community work remain the most important form? Explain your thoughts.**

**公式答（時間／金錢／人）**

I think online volunteering has very good effects. It saves a lot of time because I can do it right at home. I do not need to take a long bus ride to a far place. I can help people during my free time at night. It also saves money because there are no extra fees for gas or food. I just need my phone or my old computer. Many people in Taiwan also choose this way to help now. My friends teach English to kids online every weekend. So overall, I really think online volunteering is a great choice.

**Model Answers**

**B2 (3.5–4.5)**

Honestly, I believe the future of volunteering lies in online skill-based work because it allows non-profits to access professional expertise they could never afford otherwise. For instance, a small charity can get high-quality web design or legal advice from an expert living in a different time zone. This digital shift removes the overhead costs of housing physical volunteers and allows organizations to operate twenty-four hours a day. Plus, volunteers can build a professional portfolio by delivering real-world results like data management systems or marketing campaigns, which makes the whole process much more efficient and professional than traditional labor.

**C1 (4.5–5.0)**

I strongly believe in-person community work will remain the most vital form of volunteering due to the tangible physical impact it has on local environments. While digital work is convenient, it cannot replace the manual labor required to build houses or distribute food to those in need. Being physically present creates a sense of accountability and deep emotional bonds through face-to-face interaction that a screen simply cannot replicate. Furthermore, these hands-on projects foster community resilience by bringing neighbors together to work on shared goals, like planting a community garden. This physical effort not only improves the local area but also provides volunteers with vocational skills like carpentry while boosting their own mental health through direct social engagement.
