### Lecture 1 — The Bullwhip Effect

<audio controls src="/audio/toefl/sp05/L1.mp3"></audio>

**1.** What is the main topic of the talk?
   - (A) The role of raw material suppliers in marketing
   - (B) The history of global warehouse management
   - (C) The causes and effects of the bullwhip effect
   - (D) The benefits of increasing customer demand

**2.** The speaker states that as the original demand signal moves toward raw material suppliers, it becomes:
   - (A) More accurate due to professional verification
   - (B) A massive and artificial peak in demand
   - (C) Easier to manage via warehouse technology
   - (D) Significantly smaller due to logistical delays

**3.** What does the speaker suggest about the relationship between different sectors of the supply chain?
   - (A) They are usually located in the same geographic region
   - (B) They share too much data which leads to panic
   - (C) They prioritize consumer satisfaction over making profits
   - (D) They act independently which causes system fragility

**4.** According to the speaker, all of the following groups are involved in the over-ordering chain EXCEPT:
   - (A) Government regulators
   - (B) Retail stores
   - (C) Wholesalers
   - (D) Raw material suppliers

**Script**

Have you ever wondered why a small increase in customer demand can lead to a massive shortage of products months later? This is often caused by the 'bullwhip effect' in supply chain management. The process starts with a minor fluctuation in retail sales. When a store sees a small spike in demand, the immediate result is a slightly larger order placed with the wholesaler to ensure they don't run out. But the consequences go further than that. The wholesaler, seeing the larger order from the store, panics and places an even bigger order with the manufacturer. This sets off a chain of over-ordering at every stage of the supply chain. What many people don't realize is that by the time the message reaches the raw material suppliers, the original 'small increase' has transformed into a massive, artificial demand peak. This ripples outward to affect global shipping costs and warehouse space. Eventually, the market becomes flooded with excess inventory that no one actually wants once the initial hype dies down. This demonstrates why real-time data sharing between stores and factories is so vital for modern commerce. It makes us reflect on how fragile our global systems are when people stop talking to each other and start making guesses based on incomplete information.

   - (A) Describe the psychological discomfort caused by highly accurate prediction

**3.** According to the speaker, what difficulty arises for marketers in a 'world of big data'?
   - (A) Convincing companies to invest in block-resistant ads
   - (B) Finding enough qualified scientists to interpret the data
   - (C) Dealing with the slow processing speeds of older AI systems
   - (D) Balancing data-driven insights with the human need for privacy

**4.** The speaker mentions all of the following as components of the consumer experience EXCEPT:
   - (A) Browsing history
   - (B) Click-through rates
   - (C) Television commercials
   - (D) Privacy tools

**Script**

You've probably noticed that after you search for a pair of shoes online, ads for those exact sneakers follow you to every website you visit. This starts with a brand using AI to analyze your browsing history and predict exactly what you want to buy next. The immediate outcome is a higher click-through rate, because the ads feel relevant to your life. Then, this triggers a deeper shift in consumer behavior where people begin to expect every digital experience to be perfectly tailored to them. However, a series of unintended reactions follows. As algorithms get better at predicting our needs, it creates what experts call 'the creepiness factor.' Customers feel like they're being watched by a ghost in the machine. The surprising part is that when consumers feel too exposed, they don't just ignore the ads; they actively start using privacy tools to block the brand entirely. So, what started as a clever way to increase sales ends up destroying the very trust required to keep a customer loyal. For the marketers behind these screens, it's a reminder that even in a world of big data, people still value their personal space.
